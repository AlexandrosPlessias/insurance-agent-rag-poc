## **1. Refund Policy**

The organization maintains a conservative refund policy designed to protect both the business and its customers from fraudulent or erroneous return claims. Refunds are permitted exclusively within a 7-day window from the original date of purchase, after which no refund requests will be considered regardless of circumstance. Only cash refunds are available at this time; no alternative reimbursement methods such as store credit, bank transfers, or card reversals are supported by the current point-of-sale infrastructure. A physical receipt in its original paper form is mandatory for all refund requests without exception. Photocopies, handwritten notes, or verbal confirmations of purchase are not accepted as valid proof. Employees must verify that the receipt matches the item being returned, including product code, purchase date, and store location. All refund transactions must be processed at the same store location where the original purchase was made; crossstore refunds are not supported. Items must be returned in their original, unopened packaging with all seals, tags, and protective materials intact.
```

## **2. Theft & Incident Handling**

The organization takes a zero-tolerance approach to theft while simultaneously prioritizing the physical safety of all employees, customers, and third parties present in the store environment. In cases of suspected theft, employees are required to strictly observe the situation from a safe distance and report their observations to the duty manager as quickly and discreetly as possible. Under no circumstances are employees permitted to verbally confront, question, accuse, or engage with the suspected individual. Any form of direct intervention, including standing in doorways, following the person through aisles, or making comments intended to deter or embarrass, is strictly prohibited. Employees must not block exits, follow suspects outside the premises, attempt to recover merchandise from the individual's possession, or physically touch the person in any way. Such actions expose the organization to legal liability and create unacceptable safety risks for all parties involved. The duty manager is solely responsible for deciding whether to contact in-store security (where available) or law enforcement.
```

## **4. Documentation**

Accurate and timely documentation of incidents, complaints, and notable customer interactions is an essential operational discipline that supports accountability, trend analysis, and legal protection for both the organization and its employees. All incident reports must be completed on the standard paper forms available at the service desk. These forms are pre-printed with guided fields to help employees capture the required information in a structured and consistent manner. Reports must be filed manually in the store's designated physical filing cabinet within 24 hours of the incident. Late submissions compromise the reliability of the record and may result in a reminder from the store manager. Each report must include at minimum: the date and time of the incident, the specific store location or area where it occurred, the full name and employee ID of the person completing the report, a clear and factual description of the event, any actions taken in response, and the signature of the reviewing manager confirming the report has been read. The store manager is responsible for compiling weekly incident summaries based on the filed reports.
```

## **5. Data Protection & Privacy**

The organization recognizes the importance of customer privacy and expects all employees to treat customer information with basic confidentiality and professional discretion at all times. Customer data, including names, phone numbers, home addresses, email addresses, and any financial details shared during transactions, must not be spoken aloud in public areas where other customers or unauthorized individuals might overhear. Employees should lower their voice or move to a more private area when discussing sensitive customer details. At this time, no formal written data access protocols or digital access control systems are in place. Employees are expected to use good judgment regarding what information they access, when they access it, and for what purpose. Accessing customer data out of curiosity, for personal reasons, or for any purpose unrelated to a current business transaction is considered a breach of trust. ID verification is not currently required for refund transactions regardless of value. The organization relies on receipt verification as the primary fraud control mechanism for returns and refunds.
```
